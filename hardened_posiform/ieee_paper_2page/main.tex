\begin{abstract}
Benchmarking QUBO solvers requires instances whose optimum is known yet
that remain hard. Posiform planting provides such instances with a unique
planted optimum; a recent variant adds random spin-glass terms and scales the
planted term by a coefficient $\alpha$ that tunes hardness. Hardness, however,
is graded by the success rate, which is identically $0\%$ across the hard
regime and so cannot rank instances, solvers, or $\alpha$ values within it. We
instead measure, over failing runs only and on a fixed set of the hardest
instances, the energy gap to the optimum and the Hamming distance (HD) from
the planted answer; together they turn the flat $0\%$ into a continuous
difficulty curve. Under simulated annealing on a D-Wave Pegasus graph
($n{=}5612$) the curve is non-monotone in $\alpha$ -- a peak, then a decline
-- with the two quantities opposed: as $\alpha$ grows the failing samples move
closer in HD yet deeper in energy. A single supporting run on a D-Wave
Advantage annealer reproduces the same qualitative shape on much larger gaps,
consistent with the structure not being specific to SA. Reporting both
failure-conditioned quantities enables finer-grained grading of planted QUBO
benchmarks in the zero-success regime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Benchmarking programmable quantum annealers and classical heuristics
on QUBO problems requires instances whose optima are known yet
non-trivial to find. Posiform planting~\cite{hahn2023posiform}
produces unique-ground-state QUBOs but is easy for simulated
annealing (SA)~\cite{kirkpatrick1983}. Pelofske \emph{et al.}~\cite{pelofske2024hardened}
increased its hardness by adding random
discrete-coefficient spin-glass QUBOs $R_i$ to a posiform-planted
QUBO $P$ (unique minimum $x^\star$) through a posiform scaling
coefficient $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
  Q_{\alpha}(x) \;=\; \sum_{i} R_{i}(x) \;+\; \alpha\,P(x).
  \label{eq:qalpha}
\end{equation}
That study tuned hardness mainly through the random-QUBO size and the
annealing time, fixing $\alpha\in\{0.01,0.1\}$, and reported the
\emph{ground-state success rate}. But the success rate is identically
$0\%$ across the entire small-$\alpha$ regime, so it cannot rank
instances, solvers, or $\alpha$ values \emph{within} it. Pelofske \emph{et
al.}\ conjecture the hardness stems from ``local minima whose energy is very
close to the true ground state.''

We make that conjecture quantitative. $\Delta E$ proxies local-minimum depth,
but over all samples it conflates depth with the success rate; we therefore
condition on failure and restrict to the $100$ hardest instances, held constant
as $\alpha$ varies. So measured, failure-conditioned $\Delta E$ and Hamming
distance (HD) reveal a $\Delta R{+}\alpha\Delta P$ structure with a non-monotone
peak. Neither quantity is new; our
contribution is the protocol -- failure-conditioning on a fixed hardest set
-- that converts the uninformative $0\%$ rate into a rankable curve, with a
single supporting quantum-annealer run consistent with the structure
reflecting the instances rather than the SA implementation alone.

\section{Methodology}
\textbf{Instances.} We generate $500$ posiform-planted instances on the
D-Wave Pegasus P16 graph~\cite{boothby2020} ($n{=}5612$ qubits) with
\texttt{lin2} coefficients ($R_{ij}\in\{-1,+1\}$). Kernighan--Lin bisection
splits the graph into $\leq16$-qubit blocks, each $R_i$ solved exactly. The planted target $x^\star$
-- the concatenation of the block ground states -- is the global minimum of
$\sum_i R_i$; $P$ is the posiform of a planted 2-SAT with unique solution
$x^\star$. We rank the $500$ by total SA failing-reads across the sweep and fix the
hardest $100$ as a constant set -- chosen from SA alone before any QPU run, so
the cross-solver agreement is not circular -- then run both solvers on it.

\textbf{Metrics and flags.} Per sample $x$ we record
HD$(x,x^\star)$~\cite{hamming1950} and the energy gap
$\Delta E = E(x)-E(x^\star)\geq 0$ under the same $Q_\alpha$. With lin2
every $R$ energy is integer, so we report $\Delta E$ in units of the
minimal non-degenerate $R$ gap ($\Delta_R{=}1$), comparable across
instances, as is HD$/n$. At $\alpha{=}0$, $x^\star$ is one of many $R$ ground
states, so an energy match ($|\Delta E|<10^{-7}$) need not be a bit match; for
$\alpha{>}0$ it is unique. We score a read as a \emph{success} only on a bit-match
($x{=}x^\star$); the $0\%$ success rate and the per-read failure rate use this
criterion, not the (at $\alpha{=}0$ degenerate) ground-state energy. Since
correct samples ($\Delta E{\approx}0$, HD${=}0$)
drive both quantities to $0$ at large $\alpha$, all statistics below are
\emph{conditional on failure} ($x{\neq}x^\star$). Means are over failing reads;
the instance-level standard error stays below $0.06\,\Delta_R$ (error bars omitted).

\textbf{Protocol.} We sweep $\alpha$ over $0$, $10^{-9}$, $10^{-5}$,
$10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$, $3{\times}10^{-3}$, $5{\times}10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$,
$1.5{\times}10^{-2}$, $2{\times}10^{-2}$, refined near the peak; beyond it the
instances are increasingly solved, leaving no fixed failing set. The main sweep runs
the \texttt{neal} SA package~\cite{neal} at $1000$ sweeps, $100$ reads per
(instance,\,$\alpha$). For a cross-solver check we
replay the same $100$ instances on a D-Wave Advantage annealer
(\texttt{Advantage\_system6.4}, hardware-native), with auto-scaling, two-gauge
averaging, and $100$ reads at $20\,\mu$s; $\Delta E$ is recomputed from $Q_\alpha$.

\subsection{The $\Delta_R$ floor and the $\alpha P$ peak}
\label{sec:vpc}
At $\alpha{=}0$, $R$ is degenerate: $56.1\%$ of samples reach an $R$ ground
state ($\Delta E{=}0$) yet $0\%$ equal $x^\star$, with HD maximal ($459$,
$8.2\%$ of $n$); $\Delta E$ alone would read as solved, while only HD shows no
sample is near $x^\star$. For $\alpha{>}0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:traj}b), in the
small-$\alpha$ region ($\alpha\in[10^{-9},10^{-4}]$) $\Delta E$ stays flat at
the bare $R$ depth -- mean $1.10$, median exactly $1$ -- because $\alpha\Delta
P$ is negligible; HD is likewise flat ($\approx73$). As $\alpha$ increases past $10^{-3}$ the
planted term becomes significant and $\Delta E$ rises to a
\emph{non-monotone peak} of $3.35$ at $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ -- $3.0\times$ the
$\Delta_R$ floor -- then declines to $2.16$; floor, peak, and decline separate
by far more than the instance-level SE ($<0.06\,\Delta_R$). HD meanwhile
decreases monotonically ($73\to11$): failing samples move closer to $x^\star$
in HD even as their energy gap grows -- opposite trends on the same samples. The peak follows from $\Delta E{=}\Delta R{+}\alpha\Delta P$ with $\Delta R,\Delta P$
per-bitstring constants: as failures near $x^\star$, $\Delta P$ falls, so $\Delta E$
peaks where rising $\alpha$ stops outpacing it. The
peak is neither a survivorship nor a
selection artifact: the $100$ instances fail $\geq91\%$ of reads at every
$\alpha$ ($0\%$ ground-state success, as~\cite{pelofske2024hardened} report),
and the same non-monotone shape appears over all $500$ instances (floor
${\approx}1.1$, peak ${\approx}3.0$), the hardest set only sharpening it.

\subsection{Quantum-annealer consistency check}
\label{sec:qpu}
As supporting evidence, a single run of the same $100$ instances on a D-Wave
Advantage annealer (Fig.~\ref{fig:traj}, red) reproduces the qualitative
pattern -- $0\%$ success, a non-monotone $\Delta E$ peak near $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$
(weaker in relative terms, $1.8\times$ its floor vs SA's $3.0\times$), and
monotonic HD decrease ($501\to167$) -- on baselines an order of magnitude
above SA's ($\Delta E\approx24$, HD$\approx500$). One run cannot establish
generality but is consistent with the structure being an instance property,
not SA-specific. The peak's couplers map to Ising
$J\approx0.03$, near the device's integrated control error (ICE); we \emph{hypothesize} that
smaller problem scales would push them below ICE and flatten the peak, leaving
this to future verification.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
Failure-conditioned $\Delta E$ diagnoses difficulty (local-minimum depth) and
HD diagnoses proximity to $x^\star$, so reporting both separates how
\emph{deep} a failure is from how \emph{close} it is. The all-sample mean
(the success rate) obscures the peak, falling at larger $\alpha$ only because
more reads succeed, not because the remaining failures are shallower. $\Delta E$ also separates solvers the success rate cannot: on a separate
$150$-instance subset (not the hardest-$100$ set above) at $\alpha{=}10^{-3}$,
$1000$ vs.\ $5000$ SA sweeps (both $0\%$ success) give $\Delta E$ $1.68$
vs.\ $1.00$. Because the curve persists across
SA budgets and is echoed by the supporting QPU run, we recommend reporting
failure-conditioned $\Delta E$ (floor and peak) with HD over a fixed hardest
set whenever an $\alpha$-like parameter is swept; this provides a failure-conditioned diagnostic of
the benchmark instances -- not a single difficulty score -- though absolute
values remain solver- and schedule-dependent.

\textbf{Limitations.} The QPU evidence is a single run (two gauges, one
$20\,\mu$s anneal); the classical side is a single SA family, and instances use
one coefficient type (\texttt{lin2}) and one topology (Pegasus).

\textbf{Future work:} broader classical solvers, more annealer gauges and
anneal times, other coefficient types and topologies, and joint
HD--$\Delta E$ analysis. Code and instances:
\url{https://github.com/YIDEUNKIM/qubo_dataset}.
