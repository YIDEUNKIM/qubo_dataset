\begin{abstract}
Benchmarking quantum annealers and classical optimization heuristics requires
problem instances whose optimal solution is known in advance, yet that remain
genuinely hard to solve. Posiform planting generates
Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) instances with a single,
unique planted optimum; a hardened variant raises their difficulty by adding
random spin-glass terms and scaling the planted component by a coefficient
$\alpha$. A practical obstacle, however, is measurement: hardness is
conventionally graded by the ground-state success rate, but across the entire
hard regime this rate is identically $0\%$, so it cannot distinguish instances,
solvers, or values of $\alpha$ from one another. This poster presents a
measurement protocol that restores discrimination in the zero-success regime.
Instead of asking whether a run succeeded, we quantify how close the failing
runs come to the planted optimum, using two complementary quantities computed
over failures only and on a fixed set of the hardest instances: the energy gap
to the optimum and the Hamming distance from the planted answer. Together they
convert the flat $0\%$ rate into a continuous, rankable difficulty curve. On
instances defined over a D-Wave Pegasus graph ($n=5612$ qubits), the curve is
non-monotone in $\alpha$ -- rising to a distinct peak and then declining. A
single supporting run on a D-Wave Advantage annealer reproduces the same shape,
more weakly, consistent with the structure reflecting the instances rather than
simulated annealing alone. The protocol offers a finer-grained way to grade
planted QUBO benchmarks.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Poster Relevance to IEEE Quantum Week (QCE 2026)}
This work addresses the \emph{benchmarking, performance evaluation, and
verification of programmable quantum annealers} -- core themes of IEEE Quantum
Week. Its subject is the QUBO problem, the native input format of D-Wave
quantum annealers and a central model for quantum and quantum-inspired
optimization. The proposed failure-conditioned metrics target the NISQ-era
regime in which solvers seldom reach the true ground state and a binary success
rate is uninformative, yet still rank instances, solvers, and a
hardware-relevant tunable difficulty parameter. The study spans both classical
(simulated annealing) and quantum (D-Wave Advantage) solvers under a common
measure, supporting cross-platform comparison. It thus connects to QCE topics
in (i)~quantum benchmarking and performance characterization, (ii)~quantum
annealing and optimization, and (iii)~reproducible evaluation methodology and
tooling for quantum hardware. All code and benchmark instances are released
openly (\texttt{github.com/YIDEUNKIM/qubo\_dataset}) to support reproducibility.
